Lovable vs Bolt vs Replit: which is best for a business app?

By Taimoor Ahmed · 6 min read

Lovable, Bolt and Replit are the three AI app builders we see most often from Australian founders. All three can turn a description into a working app in minutes. But they work quite differently, and the right choice depends on what you're building and how you plan to run it.

The quick answer

  • Lovable is the easiest place for a non technical founder to get a full app with logins and a database running quickly.
  • Bolt is great for fast prototyping and gives you more freedom over the tech, but you'll set up more of the back end yourself.
  • Replit is a full development environment that builds, hosts and runs your app in one place, and suits people who want to stay with one vendor or get more hands on.

Lovable

Lovable generates a full stack web app and sets up the database, logins and file storage for you. It uses a fixed, modern stack (React and Tailwind), which keeps things consistent. It also syncs your code to GitHub, so you always have a copy you control.

Best for: founders who want a working product with user accounts as fast as possible and don't want to think about servers.

Watch out for: database permissions. Because the back end is set up for you, it's easy to assume it's locked down when it isn't. We cover this in our Lovable security checklist.

Bolt

Bolt runs your project right in the browser and supports most JavaScript frameworks, so it's flexible about how your app is built. There's no back end by default, so you connect one yourself, usually Supabase.

Best for: quick prototypes, testing ideas and teams that haven't settled on a tech stack yet.

Watch out for: the extra setup. The flexibility means more decisions, and more places for things like secret keys to end up in the wrong spot.

Replit

Replit is closer to a full online coding workspace with an AI agent built in. It can set up a database, run your app and deploy it on Replit's own cloud. It supports many languages, not just JavaScript, and lets several people work on the same project at once.

Best for: people who want everything in one place, or who are comfortable going a bit deeper into the code.

Watch out for: being tied to one platform. Make sure your code is synced to GitHub and you know how you'd move hosting if you ever need to.

What about cost?

All three have free or low cost starter plans and charge around $20 to $100 a month for serious use, based on credits or tokens. Pricing changes often, so check each site before you commit. For most businesses the subscription is the smallest cost. The bigger cost is what happens after the prototype works.

The part all three have in common

Whichever tool you pick, the same gaps tend to appear when an app moves from demo to real customers: open database rules, secret keys in the browser, no backups, no error tracking and hosting on a preview link. None of the tools are bad. They're just optimised for getting something working, not for running a business on it.
