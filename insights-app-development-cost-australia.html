How much does it cost to build an app in Australia?

By Taimoor Ahmed · 6 min read

It's the first question almost every founder asks us, and the honest answer is: it depends. But "it depends" isn't much help when you're trying to plan a budget. So here are the real ranges we see in the Australian market, what pushes a project up or down, and where you can save.

The typical price ranges

For an app built the traditional way by an Australian agency, these are the ranges you'll commonly see quoted:

  • Simple app with a handful of core features: roughly $15,000 to $50,000, taking one to three months.
  • Medium app such as a client portal, booking system or marketplace: roughly $50,000 to $150,000, taking three to six months.
  • Complex app in health, finance or with heavy AI and integrations: $150,000 and up, often six to twelve months or more.

Most of that cost is people's time. Australian agencies typically charge somewhere between $120 and $200 an hour, so every week of work adds up fast.

What actually drives the cost

  1. Number of features. Every screen, role and workflow adds design, build and testing time.
  2. Integrations. Connecting to Xero, Salesforce, payment gateways or government systems often takes longer than the app itself.
  3. Compliance. Health, NDIS and finance apps need audit trails, careful data handling and more testing.
  4. Web, mobile or both. A web app that works well on phones is usually much cheaper than separate iPhone and Android apps.
  5. How clear the requirements are. Vague scope is the number one reason projects blow out. A few days of planning up front saves weeks later.

Don't forget the running costs

Building the app is only the start. Budget for:

  • Maintenance: a common rule of thumb is 15 to 20 percent of the build cost each year for updates, security fixes and small improvements.
  • Hosting: anywhere from $50 to a few hundred dollars a month, depending on users.
  • App store fees if you publish to Apple or Google, plus any paid services like SMS, maps or payments.
  • GST on top of quotes, if your developer is registered.

How AI tools are changing the numbers

Tools like Lovable, Bolt and Replit now let founders build a working first version themselves, often for the cost of a monthly subscription. That's a big shift. Instead of paying an agency to build from scratch, many businesses now build a prototype, test it with real users, and then pay a team to make it production ready.

That last step matters. AI built apps often have gaps in security, data access and hosting that aren't obvious in a demo. Fixing them is usually a fraction of the cost of a full build, but skipping it can be very expensive later.

How to keep your costs down

  • Start with the smallest version that solves one real problem, then grow it.
  • Write down exactly what each type of user needs to do before anyone writes code.
  • Choose a web app first unless you truly need phone features like offline use or the camera.
  • Make sure the code, hosting and data are in your name, so you can change developers without starting again.
